======================================================================
0. THIS IS STAGE 1 OF 2 — SCOPE YOUR EFFORT ACCORDINGLY
======================================================================

A second researcher will receive your output afterwards. Their job is to open
every source you name, verify it, and produce frame-accurate timestamps. So do
NOT spend your effort scrubbing through videos second by second.

YOUR JOB, in priority order:

  1. Split the script into ordered visual beats (moments).
  2. FIND AND VERIFY THE ACTUAL SOURCE VIDEOS. Real URLs, opened by you, that
     play right now. This is the single most important output of this stage.
  3. Say which beat belongs to which source.
  4. Where the caption line is already obvious to you, add a DIALOGUE locator.

WHAT YOU DO **NOT** HAVE TO DO IN THIS STAGE:
  - You do NOT need EXACT_TIME timestamps for every moment. Stage 2 does that.
  - You do NOT need frame_hints. Stage 2 does that.
  - You do NOT need to scrub a whole 22-minute episode to find one shot.

Because those are removed, you have room to do the part that only live browsing
can do: finding real, playable, correct videos. Spend your budget there.

A pack with 10 verified real URLs and no timestamps is USEFUL — stage 2 can
finish it. A pack with beautiful timestamps on invented URLs is WORTHLESS, and
nothing downstream can save it.

======================================================================
1. THE ONE FAILURE THAT KILLS THIS STAGE
======================================================================

Do not write a URL you did not open.

A previous stage-1 attempt returned seven sources that looked perfectly
plausible — real-looking video IDs, real channel names, round durations — and
marked them all METADATA_ONLY because it had not actually browsed. Every
timestamp built on top of them later was worthless, and the whole video had to
be restarted.

So:
  - Open every URL. Confirm it plays, publicly, right now.
  - Copy video_id from the address bar of that exact video.
  - Copy duration_sec from that exact video's real length.
  - Copy the real channel name and real title.
  - Set inspection_status honestly:
        VERIFIED_WATCHED   = you opened it and watched some of it
        TRANSCRIPT_CHECKED = you opened it and read its captions
        METADATA_ONLY      = you only saw title/channel/description
  - Set has_captions true/false based on what you actually saw on the page.

If you cannot find a real, playable source for a section of the script, say so.
Give that pack `sources: []`, put the specific missing item in
`packs_needing_more_research`, and move on. An honest gap is recoverable. A
fabricated URL is not, and it silently poisons everything downstream.

Never output a source with inspection_status METADATA_ONLY that you did not
actually open. If you did not open it, do not include it at all.

======================================================================
2. WHAT MAKES A GOOD SOURCE
======================================================================

Prefer, in this order:
  1. Official network/studio/show channel full episode.
  2. Official channel clip.
  3. Licensed distributor upload.
  4. Clean scene upload you personally inspected.
  5. Compilation, only when the section you want is clean and the identity of the
     footage is certain.

PREFER FULL EPISODES OVER SHORT CLIPS. The engine downloads each source once and
cuts every moment from it locally, so one verified full episode serving eight
moments is far better than eight separate clips: fewer downloads, fewer dead
links, and a much larger bank of frames for the moments that never get an exact
clip. Reuse one verified source for as many non-overlapping beats as it honestly
supports.

Reject any source containing: reaction host, facecam, reviewer or narrator on
top; picture-in-picture or split screen; breakdown/review commentary; AMV, fan
edit, fan animation, speed change, mirrored footage, or vertical reframing; large
intrusive watermark, channel promo covering the subject, meme text, or heavy
burned-in subtitles; wrong show, wrong episode, or a remake when the script means
another version; visibly low-resolution or corrupted footage when a cleaner one
exists.

A small official broadcaster corner bug is acceptable if no cleaner version
exists; note it in source_notes.

PREFER SOURCES THAT HAVE CAPTIONS. Captions are how the engine finds a line
inside a video. When two uploads are otherwise equal, take the one with
subtitles and set has_captions accordingly — it makes stage 2 far more accurate.

======================================================================
3. SCRIPT SEGMENTATION
======================================================================

1. Every `script_cue_exact` must be copied verbatim from the supplied narration
   as ONE CONTINUOUS SUBSTRING. Preserve wording, order, spelling, apostrophes
   and punctuation exactly. Never summarize, never join non-contiguous sentences.
2. Make each cue distinctive — prefer wording that occurs only ONCE in the whole
   script. If a sentence repeats, extend the cue with its neighbour so the
   combined text is unique.
3. Cue length roughly 8-30 words.
4. Every narration sentence belongs to exactly one moment, in original order. No
   omitted opening, bridge or closing sentences. No sentence used twice.
5. NO MOMENT MAY EXCEED ABOUT 15 SECONDS OF NARRATION (about 40 words). Split
   long paragraphs into 2-3 beats. Long beats are how videos end up with 25-second
   stretches carrying one static image.
6. Aim for a new useful visual roughly every 8-15 seconds of narration.
7. The first script words are in the first moment; the last words in the last.

Cluster moments by show + episode/source: one pack per episode/film, containing
all the beats that come from it. Packs need not follow narration order, but the
cues together must cover the narration exhaustively.

For an episode, scope.kind is SERIES (never EPISODE). Put episode details in
scope.episode_title, scope.season and scope.episode_number.

======================================================================
4. SCOPE TITLE — ONE SHOW, ONE STRING
======================================================================

The engine groups packs by the exact text of scope.kind + scope.title. Two
spellings of one show are read as two different shows, and then one pack's
footage is never used for the other pack's moments.

  - Pick the show's canonical official title once and repeat that exact string in
    every pack for that show — same capitalisation, same punctuation, no added
    descriptors, no season suffix, no nicknames.
  - Correct:  "The Amazing World of Gumball" in P01, P02, P03...
  - Wrong:    "The Amazing World of Gumball" in P01 and
              "The Wonderfully Weird World of Gumball" in P06.
  - Genuinely different works keep different titles: "Naruto" and
    "Naruto Shippuden" are two shows.

======================================================================
5. ANALYSIS BEATS — DO NOT DUMP THE SCRIPT INTO A GRAPHIC PACK
======================================================================

Abstract analysis, thesis lines, comparisons and chapter transitions are real
beats, but they are NOT an excuse to skip research. A previous attempt put 59 of
83 moments into a GRAPHIC pack — 71% of the video with nothing to show.

Rules:
  - Keep GRAPHIC moments under 25% of total moments. If more than a quarter of
    the script is landing in the graphic pack, you are using it as a dumping
    ground for beats you did not research.
  - Most analysis lines are ABOUT something that happens on screen. If the line
    analyses a scene, make it a normal moment in that show's pack, not a card.
  - For the analysis beats that genuinely have no scene, give each one a
    fallback_plan naming which show packs its backdrop may come from:

        "fallback_plan": {
          "allowed_pack_ids": ["P01", "P02"],
          "overlay_text": "3-8 words to show on screen"
        }

    That costs no research and lets the engine put the text over a real frame of
    the show instead of a blank card. Stage 2 will add the exact frame seconds.

======================================================================
6. LOCATORS IN THIS STAGE
======================================================================

Only two locator types produce footage: EXACT_TIME and DIALOGUE. Never use
APPROX_WINDOW, SEARCH_ONLY or UNRESOLVED to make the JSON look complete — they
produce nothing at all. An empty `locators: []` is better and honest.

DIALOGUE (preferred in this stage — cheap and powerful):
    { "source_id": "P01_S01", "locator_type": "DIALOGUE",
      "dialogue_exact": "verbatim line from that source's captions",
      "nearby_context_terms": ["distinctive nearby word"],
      "verification_method": "TRANSCRIPT", "confidence": "HIGH" }
  The engine downloads that source's real captions and computes the second
  itself, so you do not need a timestamp. But the line must be VERBATIM from the
  captions — not paraphrased, not remembered, not the narrator's wording, and
  never two speakers' lines joined together. If you did not read it in the
  captions, do not write it.

EXACT_TIME (optional in this stage):
    { "source_id": "P01_S01", "locator_type": "EXACT_TIME",
      "start_sec": 412.0, "end_sec": 418.0,
      "verification_method": "WATCHED", "confidence": "HIGH" }
  Only when you genuinely saw that range in that exact upload. start_sec must be
  inside the upload's real duration, end_sec > start_sec, normally 3-9 seconds.
  Never reuse a timestamp across two different uploads of the same episode.

A moment with `locators: []` is perfectly acceptable here as long as it sits in
the right pack, so stage 2 knows exactly which video to search.

======================================================================
7. ENUM VALUES — USE THESE EXACTLY
======================================================================

scope.kind:        SERIES | FILM | DOCUMENTARY | REAL_WORLD | GRAPHIC | MULTI_SOURCE
                   (never EPISODE)
visual_role:       EXACT_SCENE | CONTEXT_SCENE | LOCAL_GRAPHIC |
                   MULTI_SOURCE_MONTAGE | STILL_FROM_VERIFIED_SOURCE
                   (never TEXT_CARD/GRAPHIC/MONTAGE/B_ROLL as a visual_role)
source_kind:       OFFICIAL_EPISODE | OFFICIAL_CLIP | LICENSED_UPLOAD |
                   CLEAN_SCENE | COMPILATION | OTHER
                   (never OFFICIAL_FULL_EPISODE)
inspection_status: VERIFIED_WATCHED | TRANSCRIPT_CHECKED | METADATA_ONLY
locator_type:      EXACT_TIME | DIALOGUE | APPROX_WINDOW | SEARCH_ONLY | UNRESOLVED
confidence:        HIGH | MEDIUM | LOW | NONE
fallback.type:     TEXT_CARD | LOCAL_GRAPHIC | NEEDS_SOURCE

IDs: pack_id, source_id, moment_id are globally unique, only A-Z a-z 0-9 _ -,
pattern P01, P01_S01, P01_M01. Every locator source_id must exist in some pack's
sources[]. Never source_id:null.
