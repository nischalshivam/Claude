0. THE FIVE MISTAKES THAT RUINED PREVIOUS PACKS — READ FIRST
======================================================================

These are not hypothetical. Each one was measured on a real delivered pack and
each one destroyed a large part of the finished video. Everything else in this
prompt exists to prevent them.

MISTAKE 1 — Counting moments instead of seconds.
  A previous pack had evidence for 65% of its moments and looked fine. But the
  moments without evidence were the LONG ones: 29 moments consumed 328 seconds,
  39% of the whole video, with no footage behind them. Coverage is measured in
  SECONDS of narration, never in moment count. A 25-second analysis beat matters
  five times more than a 5-second one.

MISTAKE 2 — Giving only DIALOGUE and no EXACT_TIME.
  A previous pack had 46 moments whose only evidence was a DIALOGUE locator. The
  engine finds dialogue timing by downloading that source's real captions — but
  many YouTube uploads have no usable captions at all. Result: only 7.6% of the
  narration had evidence that could survive without captions, and the finished
  video showed exact scenes for roughly a tenth of its length.
  => Whenever you have verified a moment, give BOTH a DIALOGUE locator and an
     EXACT_TIME locator for the same source. Two independent ways to find the
     same instant. This single habit is the highest-value thing in this prompt.

MISTAKE 3 — Writing the same show's title two different ways.
  One pack used "The Amazing World of Gumball" and another used "The Wonderfully
  Weird World of Gumball". The engine compares scope.title as a plain string, so
  it treated these as two different shows and refused to use one pack's episodes
  for the other pack's moments. That alone pushed 29% of the video onto
  borrowed/blank visuals.
  => One show = ONE canonical title string, character-for-character identical in
     every pack, every time.

MISTAKE 4 — Leaving analysis/thesis beats with no visual evidence.
  Abstract analysis lines are usually 25-35% of a video essay. A previous pack
  put 19 such moments in a GRAPHIC pack with sources:[] and locators:[], so the
  engine had nothing to draw and fell back to plain text on a flat background.
  => A GRAPHIC/analysis moment does NOT need its own video sources, but it MUST
     carry a fallback_plan that names which packs it may borrow frames from and
     the exact seconds of good frames. Then the analysis text appears over a real
     frame of the real show. Details in section 8.

MISTAKE 5 — Timestamps outside the video's real length.
  A 22-minute episode cannot have a scene at start_sec 1400 if the upload is only
  1200 seconds long. Read the real duration of every upload before writing any
  timestamp, and record it in duration_sec.

1. WHAT THE ENGINE ACTUALLY DOES WITH YOUR OUTPUT
======================================================================

Understanding this tells you what is worth researching.

The engine is fully deterministic. It has NO image search, NO semantic
understanding, NO AI vision, and NO ability to find footage on its own. It can
only do what your JSON points it at. Specifically it can:

1. Download an approved source once at <=720p, then cut every moment out of it
   locally.
2. Turn EXACT_TIME into a clip directly.
3. Turn DIALOGUE into a clip by downloading that source's real captions and
   fuzzy-matching your dialogue_exact against them to compute the true second.
   (This is why dialogue is powerful when captions exist, and worthless when they
   do not.)
4. Extract a bank of still frames from any source it already downloaded, and use
   them for stills, Ken-Burns shots, montages, and as the backdrop behind text.
5. For a moment with no clip, cut a short CONTEXT clip from another approved
   source of the SAME show (correct show, not the exact scene).
6. Place text over one of those real frames.

It will never: search YouTube, judge whether a face is the right character,
detect a reaction host, pick "a good-looking frame", or invent artwork.

Every visual therefore traces back to something you named. Two locator types
become real clips: EXACT_TIME and DIALOGUE. APPROX_WINDOW, SEARCH_ONLY and
UNRESOLVED produce nothing — never use them to make the JSON look complete.

fallback.type values that mean something: TEXT_CARD, LOCAL_GRAPHIC, NEEDS_SOURCE.
Do not use STILL_FROM_PACK, ANOTHER_VERIFIED_MOMENT or MULTI_SOURCE_MONTAGE as a
fallback.type — express those intentions through fallback_plan (section 8).

2. NON-NEGOTIABLE ANTI-HALLUCINATION RULES
======================================================================

1.  Never invent a YouTube URL, video ID, title, channel, duration, episode name,
    timestamp, caption, spoken line, or verification status.
2.  Open every included YouTube URL. Confirm it resolves to the stated video and
    is publicly playable right now (not private, deleted, or region-locked).
3.  video_id must be copied from that exact URL and must match the metadata.
4.  duration_sec must be the real length of that exact upload, in seconds.
5.  A timestamp from one upload must never be reused for another upload. Episode
    uploads differ by intro length, ads, and trimming.
6.  EXACT_TIME means you actually watched/verified that range in that upload.
7.  DIALOGUE means you actually found that line in that source's captions.
8.  dialogue_exact must be copied from the source's captions verbatim. Not
    paraphrased, not remembered, not reconstructed, and never the narrator's own
    words. If the caption reads "I'm not mad, I'm disappointed", write exactly
    that — not "she says she is disappointed rather than angry".
9.  Do not join two caption lines spoken by different people, or lines separated
    by other dialogue, into one dialogue_exact. One continuous captioned utterance.
10. VERIFIED_WATCHED = you inspected the visual. TRANSCRIPT_CHECKED = you
    inspected captions. METADATA_ONLY = you only read title/channel/description.
    A METADATA_ONLY source must never support a HIGH-confidence locator.
11. If evidence is unavailable, return an honest empty locators array plus a
    fallback_plan. An honest gap is recoverable; a plausible guess is not.
12. Do not claim entire_script_covered=true merely because every narration line
    has a moment object.

3. SOURCE QUALITY AND DISTRACTION FILTER
======================================================================

Prefer in this order:

1. Official network/studio/show channel clip or official episode.
2. Licensed distributor upload.
3. Clean scene upload that you personally inspected.
4. Compilation only when the exact range is clean and source identity is certain.

Reject any proposed range containing: reaction host, facecam, reviewer, or
narrator visible on top; picture-in-picture or split screen; breakdown/review
commentary; AMV, fan edit, fan animation, speed change, mirrored footage, crops
that remove the action, or vertical/mobile reframing; large intrusive watermark,
channel promo covering the subject, meme text, or heavy burned-in subtitles;
wrong show, wrong episode, or a remake/reboot when the script means another
version; trailer montage where a clean continuous scene is required; visibly
low-resolution or corrupted footage when a cleaner source exists.

A small official broadcaster corner bug is acceptable only if no cleaner
official/licensed version exists; note it briefly in source_notes.

PREFER FEWER, LONGER, RICHER SOURCES. The engine downloads each unique source
once and cuts every moment from it locally, so ten moments from one verified
full episode cost far less and fail far less often than ten moments from ten
different short clips. A full episode upload also gives the engine a large frame
bank to draw stills and context shots from. Reuse the same verified source for
as many non-overlapping moments as it honestly supports.

For each important moment, find one strong primary source and at most two
genuinely useful alternates. No weak filler alternatives.

4. SCRIPT SEGMENTATION AND EXACT COVERAGE
======================================================================

Before searching, segment the narration internally into ordered visual beats.

1. Every script_cue_exact must be copied verbatim from the supplied narration as
   ONE CONTINUOUS SUBSTRING. Preserve wording, order, spelling, apostrophes and
   punctuation exactly. Never summarize, never join non-contiguous sentences.
   The engine locates each moment by finding this text inside the narration
   subtitle file; if it is paraphrased or stitched, the visual lands on the wrong
   line or is dropped.
2. Make each cue distinctive. Prefer a cue whose wording occurs only ONCE in the
   whole script. If a sentence repeats, extend the cue with the neighbouring
   sentence so the combined text is unique.
3. Cue length: roughly 8-30 words. Very short cues ("Now you know why.") are
   ambiguous; very long cues waste the beat.
4. Every narration sentence must belong to exactly one moment, in original order.
   Do not omit bridge, opening, or closing sentences. Do not duplicate a sentence
   across moments.
5. A beat is normally 1-3 consecutive sentences expressing one visual idea. Aim
   for a new useful visual roughly every 8-15 seconds of narration.
6. NO BEAT SHOULD EXCEED ABOUT 15 SECONDS OF NARRATION (about 40 words). Long
   paragraphs must be split into 2-3 beats. A single long beat is exactly how
   previous packs ended up with 25-second stretches carrying no evidence.
7. The first script words must be covered by the first moment and the final
   script words by the final moment.
8. Packs may be ordered by source/show/episode rather than narration order, but
   the full set of cues must still cover the narration exhaustively.
9. Cross-show scripts are allowed. Each show/film/documentary/real-world subject
   gets its own pack(s).

For an episode, scope.kind is SERIES, never EPISODE. Put episode details in
scope.episode_title, scope.season and scope.episode_number when verified.

======================================================================
8. fallback_plan — REQUIRED ON EVERY MOMENT
======================================================================

fallback_plan tells the engine what to show when it cannot cut the exact clip.
Without it, the engine's only remaining option is plain text on a flat
background. With it, the moment still shows the right show, the right character,
often the right scene.

Shape:

"fallback_plan": {
  "allowed_pack_ids": ["P01", "P02"],
  "allowed_source_ids": ["P01_S01", "P02_S01"],
  "frame_hints": [
    { "source_id": "P01_S01", "time_sec": 132.5, "reason": "Nicole beside the fridge chart" },
    { "source_id": "P02_S01", "time_sec": 47.0,  "reason": "Nicole exhausted at the table" }
  ],
  "must_show": ["Nicole Watterson"],
  "must_not_show": ["reaction host", "wrong show", "fan art"],
  "template": "QUOTE",
  "overlay_text": "Nothing was ever enough."
}

Rules:

1. allowed_pack_ids limits which packs' footage may appear on this moment. This
   is the scope lock that prevents one show's footage appearing under another
   show's narration. Never list a pack belonging to a different subject.
2. allowed_source_ids must be sources that live inside allowed_pack_ids.
3. frame_hints are the single most valuable field you can add. The engine has no
   image search: it cannot find "a shot of the mother looking tired". It CAN jump
   to second 412 of an episode you already approved. So give it the seconds.
   - 2-3 hints per weak moment.
   - Each hint is a second where you actually saw something useful.
   - Prefer clear, well-lit, character-visible frames; avoid transitions, black
     frames, credits and title cards.
4. overlay_text is the short on-screen line (3-8 words). Copy it from the script
   or the verified source — never invent a quote.
5. template COMPARISON makes the engine build a 2-3 frame montage, so supply at
   least 2-3 frame_hints with it.
6. `keyframe_queries` is ignored by this engine. Do not rely on it. Give seconds.

WHEN A MOMENT HAS A GOOD LOCATOR: still include a minimal fallback_plan with
allowed_pack_ids and allowed_source_ids. Uploads die and captions go missing; the
fallback_plan is what keeps that beat on the right show anyway.

that passage; never all of them.

If you genuinely cannot supply frame_hints for an analysis moment, still supply
allowed_pack_ids. That alone lets the engine pull a correct-show frame instead of
a blank card.
